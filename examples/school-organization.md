# School organization implementation example

**Course:** Ikastetxearen Antolakuntza / Organización del Centro Educativo, code 25864.  
**Context:** second year of the Primary Education degree, Faculty of Education of Bilbao, Universidad del País Vasco (EHU).  
**Course edition:** 2026–2027.  
**Example updated:** 2026-11-20.  
**Scope:** documented choices from Ant_26.27. This account illustrates selected modules; it is not a complete reproduction of its materials or a required folder structure.

## Course purpose and pedagogical design

The course helps future primary teachers understand the school as a complex, situated, collective and changing organization. Its professional purpose extends an initially classroom-centred view of teaching toward the organizational conditions in which teaching takes place.

Its design follows constructivist, sociocultural, systemic, critical and professionally oriented principles. Learners progressively describe, distinguish, relate, interpret, diagnose, compare alternatives, justify decisions, plan, evaluate and revise. Diagnosis precedes proposals; the same intervention can have different effects in different school configurations.

The course has 6 ECTS and a reference workload of 150 hours. Final learner materials are in Basque, while design documentation is normally developed in Spanish. Face-to-face teaching is supported by eGela. These are local choices, not requirements of PEDAGOGY.md.

## Stable content architecture and teaching route

| Block | Main purpose | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Understand the school as an organization within its system. | C01–C04 |
| B2 | Interpret context, organizational configuration and operating conditions. | C05–C06 |
| B3 | Understand agents, relationships, decisions, culture and leadership. | C07–C10 |
| B4 | Diagnose, plan, evaluate, improve and develop organizational learning. | C11–C14 |

The conceptual order remains separate from the teaching route for an edition. Sessions can combine or revisit chapter sections around projects, placement experiences and timetable needs. Presentations select and visualize content; they need not reproduce whole chapters.

Local identifiers distinguish blocks, chapters, sections, presentations, sessions, concepts, resources, activities, projects and cases. They support specific relationships and reuse. Their prefixes are a local convention. A complete matrix connecting all components was still being developed.

## Optional ontology and resources

The ontology consolidates one identity per concept with preferred terms, access definitions, categories, sources, locations and relationships. Compatible contextual reformulations retain their identity; homonyms and nearby meanings can be distinguished. Retired identifiers are not reused.

The teacher reports no direct manual use of the ontology and considers its availability useful as context for work with AI and alignment. This describes perceived usefulness; it does not establish model retrieval or an effect on learning.

The resource spreadsheet separates sources from their uses. A source can ground, illustrate or contextualize several content objects without duplication. Identity, relevance and currency are distinct checks. Live institutional and legal resources require review before reuse; historical records retain their identity. Structural controls do not replace source verification.

The [ontology guide](../docs/ontology.md) offers a smaller starting point.

## Persistent cases as contextual infrastructure

The cases depict fictional but plausible schools, each combining context, structures, agents, resources, culture and capacity for improvement.

| Case | Configuration used for comparison |
|---|---|
| Astigar | Public school with strong community relationships, stability and gradual adaptation. |
| Belaia | Large public school facing high complexity, turnover and reactive coordination. |
| Campuzano | Large publicly funded private school with formalized roles, procedures and systematic coordination. |
| Distiñe | Cooperative ikastola with strong identity and resources, experiencing accumulated change and organizational strain. |

Each case has common organizational dimensions, a systemic synthesis and challenges. Narrative data, actor perspectives and interpretations are distinguished. The same case can be revisited as learners acquire more precise concepts. The case provides context; the tasks using it define expected learning and evidence.

The design planned complete initial access to Astigar and Distiñe and progressive presentation of Belaia and Campuzano. This describes the documented plan, not verified delivery in every session. Disclosure and scaffolding should suit the learner profile.

The teacher reports that the case system has been useful for this course. The [ICT configuration](ict-primary-education.md) intentionally omits it. An exercise can contain a scenario without requiring cases.md or cases/.

## Activity design and learner materials

The guide distinguishes internal design rules from the worksheet learners receive. It connects purpose, canonical content, competences, observable outcomes, task, resources, output, criteria and AI conditions. An internal inventory can hold additional metadata without expanding every worksheet.

Ant uses activity types including TP, P, Q and projects, with different assessment functions. A worksheet reflects the relevant sufficient-performance criteria or rubric. Assessment follows the validated course design; the example does not introduce new grading rules.

The AI policy becomes specific in each activity. When authorized use must be declared, the declaration records purpose, use, tool and checks or changes. Disclosure alone is not evidence of learning. Local codes, scales and permissions should be adapted to each course.

## Maintenance and evidence

Changes to shared concepts, content architecture or pedagogical rules identify related materials that need updating. Editorial changes can stay local. Core pedagogy, developed content, ontology access definitions and source metadata have different responsibilities within the teacher's decisions.

Documented counts and module existence establish development and structural properties, not educational effectiveness. The teacher reports no problems and was familiar with the capabilities and available files. Integration of all component relationships and evaluation of effects remain distinct tasks.

## Reusable patterns

Reusable patterns include selective adoption, stable concept identities, a teaching route distinct from the content map, justified relationships, concise learner materials and traceable updates. Fourteen chapters, four schools, local activity names, assessment arrangements and language choices belong to this implementation.
